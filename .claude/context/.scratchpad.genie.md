## The discovery lever — the most useful thing learned, replicated

| query | accepted rates/paper |
|---|---|
| `nitrogenase` broad | **0.009** |
| `(ARA OR nitrogenase activity) AND nmol` | **0.245**, replicated **0.236** |

**~128x. The discriminator is the BIOMASS DENOMINATOR, not the topic.**
`nmol C2H4 mg protein-1 h-1` vs `nmol N L-1 d-1` differ only there, and only the
first can label a strain. **Rank GPU spend by denominator co-mention.**

## Adjudication rules — all in `scripts/table_extract.py`, ~20 of them

Order is load-bearing: **fabrication first**, before anything that could excuse
it. Then sci-notation/percent, attribution (whitelist not blacklist),
what-the-number-is, usability (flag not reject), duplicates, **magnitude
plausibility last**. `--adjudicate-only` re-applies rules with NO new inference.

**Magnitude flags, never rejects** — a value outside its class can be an
extraction error OR a publication error. One source really does state
`~14 mmol C2H4 mg protein-1 24 h-1` (implausible by 3-4 orders, read correctly).

**The unit parser lives ONCE, in `table_extract.py`.** Two copies already cost
one bug; then I caused a second by assuming `_norm` folded Greek mu and eta —
it only had the micro sign, and parse rate fell to 76% until fixed.

**The adjudicated file is the AUTHORITY.** Reading it alongside the raw run
output re-admitted six freshly-flagged rates carrying stale verdicts.

## Standing lesson, now five-for-five

A metric measured on a curated sample has NOT transferred to an uncurated one:
NCBI uniqueness, PMC recovery, the 30-paper gold set blind to 42% contamination,
the adjudicator at 0.94 then 0.68, and a yield estimate that fell 26x when the
query widened. **Validate on the population you will run on.**

Corollaries earned the hard way: **an extraction count is a hypothesis, not a
result** (861->679->118->32). **A zero that surprises you is a bug until proven
otherwise.** **Parsing a unit is a far stronger check than matching a cue in it.**

## Older, still standing

- No published study reports a correlation between N-free growth rate and
  fixation rate across free-living diazotroph strains. Absence is STRUCTURAL —
  N-free growth is a qualitative gate applied BEFORE assaying, so range
  restriction destroys the correlation. False-positive half IS measured: 17%
  nifH+ of 442 isolates (Koirala 2025 `10.3390/microorganisms13010096`); 79%
  best case (Mirza & Rodrigues 2012 `10.1128/aem.00714-12`).
- Strongest argument against the proxy: growth rate is dominated by **O2-management
  overhead**, not fixation cost (Inomura 2017 `10.1038/ismej.2016.97`; Oelze 2000)
  — systematic bias, not noise.
- **Recommendation**: measure growth-rate ~ ¹⁵N₂ on the isolate panel, report r²
  whatever the sign. ARA is NOT a safe reference standard: ARA:N₂ spans two
  orders of magnitude across 253 ratios, ~30% calibrate (Soper 2021
  `10.1007/s10533-021-00761-3` — **still snippet-only, is_oa=false, no route**).
- Bloch 2020 (`10.1093/jxb/eraa176`) WAS retrieved and read. The "no such
  correlation" snippet is about **fertilizer vs maize yield**, NOT the proxy —
  must not be cited as decisive. It IS usable as a mechanistic argument the
  other way (ΔnifL::Prm1 strains show a growth-rate cost).
- `nfixdb_strainexact_rates.csv` — full header, **zero rows**.
- **No medium/condition column** in `dataset_master.csv` or
