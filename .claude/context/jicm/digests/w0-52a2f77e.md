# Session Summary

## Key Findings

### R2.2f: Per-Segment R4 Labeling
- **Pre-registered** in `OCR-ROADMAP.md` under "R2.2f PRE-REGISTRATION"
- **Implemented** in `witness/region_head.py` with `R4_PER_SEGMENT` flag (default `False`)
- **Failed to meet criteria** (G1, G2), particularly failing to correctly label the "pinces are" entry as MarginNote
- **Diagnosis**: The body segment on leaf 412 r2 exceeded the flush tolerance by 12px, causing the fallback to keep the note as MainText
- **Defect in scorer**: Float comparisons used `1e-9` tolerance against 4-dp literals, leading to false negatives
- **Conclusion**: R2.2f was **refuted** and the chain's prerequisite direction was corrected

### R2.2g: Flush Mode Variants
- **Pre-registered** with three flush modes: `"both"`, `"reach"`, and `"reach_right"`
- **H4 criterion** passed, resolving the cycle between R2.2f and R2.2g
- **H1 criterion** failed at 37/43, indicating a fourth cause for the remaining 6 rows
- **Diagnosis**: Leaf 409's left-edge histogram was smeared across 80px, with the modal edge tolerance window only 13px wide
- **Conclusion**: R2.2g was **refuted** but helped break the cycle, allowing further investigation

### R2.2h: Edge Estimator Fixed Point
- **Pre-registered** to address the issue of two tolerances for one edge
- **J1–J7 criteria** were defined, with J1 failing at 37/43
- **Diagnosis**: Leaf 409's row clustering issue was due to printed lines being split into two rows due to tilt
- **Conclusion**: R2.2h was **refuted** but identified a deeper issue in row clustering

### R2.2i: Row Clustering
- **Identified** as the root cause: printed lines being split into two rows due to tilt
- **Census**: 22 split pairs in 1242 rows, concentrated on leaves 409, 419, 403, and 411
- **Impact**: Neither half of a split line could be identified as a body row, leading to labeling issues
- **Conclusion**: R2.2i is the root cause and is now the next step to address

### R2.2j: Addressing Scheme
- **Pre-registered** to address the issue of row ordinals not being reliable addresses
- **M1–M3 criteria** were defined, with M1 showing the ordinal address collapsing under renaming
- **Adopted**: `ROW_ADDRESS = "ink2d"` as it addresses ink position in both axes, not row ordinals
- **Impact**: Region gold numbers were verified to be unchanged under the new addressing scheme
- **Conclusion**: R2.2j was **adopted** and is now part of the verification standard

## Lessons Learned

1. **Cycle Detection**: Scoring links one at a time can hide cycles. Only an all-flags-on criterion showed that R2.2f and R2.2g together form a repair.
2. **Instrument Validity**: The deciding criterion is always the one asking whether the instrument could fail. E3, G5, and M1 were critical in identifying issues.
3. **Addressing Scheme**: Row ordinals are not reliable addresses. The address is the ink's position on the page in both axes.
4. **Instrument Failures**: Instrument failures can lead to false negatives and incorrect conclusions. Careful validation is essential.

The session concluded with the adoption of R2.2j and the identification of R2.2i as the next step to address the root cause of row clustering issues.
